Projects at Cardiff University

Categories and Classifying Spaces | 80%

Investigated a relationship within simplicial homology, proving that the simplicial homology groups of a group are isomorphic to the simplicial homology groups of its classifying space. Used the nerve construction to associate a simplicial set to a group viewed as a category and, following Hatcher (2002), proved this has a natural Delta-complex structure. Verified the theory through computations such as the classifying space of the cyclic group of order n. Also extended the study to cohomology and applied the Universal Coefficient Theorem to relate the cohomology and homology groups of the classifying space.

Lie Groups and their relation to Lie Algebras | 90%

Explored the relationship between Lie groups and Lie algebras, focusing on tangent spaces, exponential maps, and Lie algebra isomorphisms. Studied SO(3) and SU(2) as concrete examples, demonstrating through explicit Lie bracket calculations that non-isomorphic Lie groups can have isomorphic Lie algebras. Discussed applications of Lie groups in representation theory, differential geometry, and physics.

Lie Algebras Coursework | 92%

Studied the structure of the symplectic Lie algebra sp(2n), determining its dimension and constructing a Cartan subalgebra. Calculated the corresponding root system, coroots, and Killing form through explicit matrix calculations. Constructed an sl(2)-triple within sp(2n) and verified the required commutation relations directly.

Shortest Path Between Doctors and Patients | 72%

A Python group project finding efficient routes between doctors and patients. Used local search and the Great Deluge algorithm to iteratively improve randomly generated routes while considering constraints on the number of patients assigned to each doctor. Also used GPS data and the Haversine formula to apply the algorithms to real-world geographical locations. Used NetworkX and matplotlib to visualise routes and analyse the convergence and performance of the optimisation algorithms.

Statistical Distribution Sampling Library | 100%

A Python library developed as part of a group project for generating random samples from a range of statistical distributions: the normal, exponential, Poisson, and continuous and discrete uniform distributions. It allows users to input distribution parameters and random seeds and can also be used to generate probability density and cumulative distribution plots using matplotlib. As an example, we modelled the throws of a darts player using independent normal distributions to represent their horizontal and vertical accuracy.
